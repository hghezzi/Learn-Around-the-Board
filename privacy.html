Privacy Notice

Learn Around the Board · last updated October 2026

The short version

The game runs entirely in your web browser. There are no accounts and no game server. Nothing you type or answer in the game is sent to us. In an optional online game, the devices of that game connect to each other (see below). Your results reach your instructor only if you send them (see below).

What stays on your computer

  • The question files (.tsv or .lock) and any images you upload.
  • Every answer, the pre- and post-game surveys, the in-game log, and the names or student IDs typed on the end screen.
  • The results file (CSV) you download. It is created on your computer and only goes where you choose to send it, for example your course page or your instructor's email.

What the game stores in your browser

  • Autosave. While a session is running, the game saves it in this browser only (its local storage), so it can be resumed after an accidental refresh. The save, which includes the names typed on the end screen, is deleted when the results are sent or downloaded, when you return to the main menu or exit the game, and it expires after 12 hours. It is never sent anywhere. Uploaded images are not saved.
  • Solo personal bests: the best net worth (and share of right answers) reached in solo games with each question file and session length, so the end screen can show it. It never leaves your browser; clearing this site's data removes it.
  • Your analytics choice (allow or no thanks), so the question isn't asked on every visit.
  • An offline copy of the game (its code, fonts, example questions and images, through a service worker), so it works without internet after the first visit. It contains no personal data, and installing the game as an app gives it no extra access to your device. Clearing this site's data in your browser removes all of the above.

Who else sees a request

  • The game is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub receives your IP address and browser details when the page loads (see GitHub's privacy statement).
  • If you open a game link from your instructor, your browser downloads the question file and any images from wherever the instructor keeps them (for example Google Sheets or GitHub). That host sees an ordinary web request, but nothing about your answers.

Online games (only if you choose one)

When a host chooses On their own devices, or you choose Join an online game, the game connects the devices in that game. To find each other, the devices use the free PeerJS connection service (0.peerjs.com) and public STUN/TURN servers run by Google and PeerJS. These see each device's IP address and the room code, but not the game. The game itself (the board, answers, surveys and names typed for the host) then travels directly between the devices in the game, encrypted by the browser (WebRTC); if a network doesn't allow a direct connection, it may pass, still encrypted, through a PeerJS relay. Devices never receive the answers to a question before it is answered. The host's computer collects the results as in any other game. A player's device remembers a random device code for each room it joined, so a refresh gets its player back. Nothing of this happens in a game played on one computer.

Sending results to your instructor

Only when your instructor's question file includes a results link does the end screen offer Send results to instructor. Clicking it sends the session's results (every answer, survey response and transaction, the scores, and the names or student IDs typed on that screen) directly to a Google Sheet that belongs to your instructor; Google processes this data for your instructor. Nothing is sent unless you click Send, and it is never sent to the game's authors.

The Email results to instructor button only downloads the results file and opens your own email app with a draft. You choose whether to send it.

Optional analytics

If you click Allow analytics, the site loads Google Analytics 4 to count visits: pages viewed (without the link's settings, such as a game link's question-file address), approximate location (country/region, which Google derives from your IP address; Google Analytics 4 does not store IP addresses), browser and device type. This helps us understand how widely the game is used. Google signals, advertising and personalisation features are turned off. Google Analytics sets first-party cookies (_ga, _ga_…) that let it recognise a returning browser; we limit them to about 13 months. Analytics never receives question content, answers, survey responses, file contents or names.

If you click No thanks, Google Analytics is not loaded at all. The game works the same either way.

Your choice is saved in your browser. To change it, click Analytics settings at the bottom of the game's start page, or clear this site's data in your browser. Choosing No thanks after allowing switches analytics off straight away and deletes its cookies. Google's handling of analytics data is described in Google's Privacy Policy. Analytics data may be processed on servers outside your country.

For instructors

If your institution has rules about student data (for example, privacy impact assessments or data-residency requirements), you can ask students to choose No thanks. Game data leaves students' computers only when they click Send results to instructor (to your own Google Sheet) or send the results file themselves. The Instructor Guide's privacy section has more detail. If Google services aren't allowed for student data, use the email option or a course-page upload instead, and ask students to type student numbers or initials rather than full names if your rules require it.

The question-writer skill for Claude

The optional skill that helps instructors write question files runs inside the instructor's own Claude conversation (claude.ai, the Claude app or Claude Code). Notes, slides or other files shared there are read by Claude and handled by Anthropic under the instructor's Claude account terms and privacy settings; this project never receives them, and the skill's own scripts make no network connections. Don't share sensitive data with Claude: remove student names, numbers, emails, grades and other personal information, and leave out confidential material such as live exams or licensed test banks. Where an institution provides its own Claude account, use it and follow its rules.

Who is responsible

Learn Around the Board is a free, non-commercial project by Hans Ghezzi. The project only ever receives the usage analytics described above. Results sent with Send results go to your instructor's own Google Sheet, so your instructor (and their institution) is responsible for them; ask them about access, correction or deletion.
